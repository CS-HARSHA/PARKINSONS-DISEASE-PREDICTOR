PRESENTATION PLAN
==================

Project title
-------------
An Explainable Machine Learning Pipeline for Parkinson's Screening Using Keystroke Dynamics

Subtitle: Cross-cohort evaluation of hold-time variability and MACD

The presentation should explain the full machine-learning story: why keystroke timing may contain a motor-control signal, how the data are cleaned, what the EDA shows, why MACD is selected, how the model is evaluated, and what the exploratory model comparison found.

Focus on the machine-learning pipeline. Present the webapp as future integration rather than the central contribution.

Recommended length: 12–18 minutes and approximately 12–17 slides.

SLIDE 1 — TITLE
---------------
Title: An Explainable Machine Learning Pipeline for Parkinson's Screening Using Keystroke Dynamics
Subtitle: Cross-cohort evaluation of hold-time variability and MACD

Include team members, institution/course, PhysioNet neuroQWERTY MIT-CSXPD dataset, and date.

Introduce this as a research and machine-learning demonstration, not a medical diagnostic system. The project investigates whether timing patterns in keyboard typing can provide an interpretable signal associated with Parkinson's disease.

SLIDE 2 — PROBLEM MOTIVATION
----------------------------
Title: Why keystroke timing?

Typing contains motor-control information beyond the words being typed. Focus on key hold time, change in hold time between consecutive keys, timing variability, and differences between controls and PD subjects.

Suggested example:

Key A hold time = 0.12 s
Key B hold time = 0.18 s
Absolute change = |0.18 - 0.12| = 0.06 s

The project does not analyse typed words. It uses timing information only. The hypothesis is that motor impairment may appear as reduced consistency or larger changes in successive key timings.

SLIDE 3 — RESEARCH QUESTION AND HYPOTHESIS
------------------------------------------
Research question:
Can simple, interpretable features extracted from keystroke hold times distinguish PD subjects from control subjects across independent study cohorts?

Hypothesis:
PD subjects may demonstrate higher mean hold times, greater timing variability, larger absolute changes between consecutive hold times, and higher MACD values.

Make clear that this is an association and screening research question, not a diagnosis question.

SLIDE 4 — DATASET
------------------
Dataset: PhysioNet neuroQWERTY MIT-CSXPD

MIT-CS1PD: Early-PD cohort, 31 subjects, 62 sessions.
MIT-CS2PD: De novo, untreated-PD cohort, 54 subjects, 54 sessions.
Total: 85 subjects and 116 valid sessions.

The data contain keyboard event timings, hold durations in seconds, and subject-level PD/control labels.

A cohort is a group of subjects collected under one study protocol. The project keeps cohorts separate during evaluation to test transfer between study populations.

SLIDE 6 — DATA CLEANING
-----------------------
The pipeline removes invalid rows, negative hold times, unrealistically long hold times, invalid timestamps, time-order reversals, mouse events, modifier keys, Backspace events, and sessions with fewer than 20 valid keystrokes.

The cleaning stage writes data/processed/cleaning_summary.json so removal decisions are auditable.

Cleaning is separated from feature engineering so the team can inspect what was removed before calculating features.

SLIDE 8 — EDA: CONSECUTIVE CHANGES AND MACD MOTIVATION
-------------------------------------------------------
Show results/figures/consecutive_change_by_label.png and results/figures/macd_by_label.png.

Consecutive changes:
MIT-CS1PD: Control 0.0313 s; PD 0.0511 s.
MIT-CS2PD: Control 0.0266 s; PD 0.0429 s.

MACD:
MIT-CS1PD: Control 0.0242 s; PD 0.0469 s.
MIT-CS2PD: Control 0.0221 s; PD 0.0368 s.

The PD groups show larger subject-level changes between consecutive hold times. MACD summarizes this behaviour into one interpretable feature.

MACD is not simply hold-time standard deviation. It measures average absolute change between consecutive hold times within a defined corridor.

SLIDE 9 — MACD FEATURE DEFINITION
---------------------------------
For hold times h1 through hn:

1. Calculate the session's 60th percentile.
2. Keep hold times from the 60th percentile to the session maximum.
3. Consider adjacent pairs only when both values are in the corridor.
4. Calculate the mean absolute difference.

MACD = average absolute change between valid consecutive hold times.

Example:
Valid hold times: 0.12, 0.18, 0.15, 0.22.
Absolute changes: 0.06, 0.03, 0.07.
MACD = (0.06 + 0.03 + 0.07) / 3 = 0.0533 seconds.

The corridor focuses the feature on the upper part of the hold-time distribution and reduces the influence of very short or potentially noisy events.

SLIDE 10 — SUBJECT-LEVEL AGGREGATION
------------------------------------
Keystrokes -> session hold-time sequence -> session MACD -> subject-level average MACD -> model input.

A subject may contribute multiple sessions. The pipeline calculates MACD per session, groups sessions by subject, averages session values, and evaluates at subject level.

All sessions from one subject stay in the same train or test split. This prevents subject-specific leakage.

SLIDE 11 — CROSS-COHORT EVALUATION
----------------------------------
Fold 1: Train on MIT-CS1PD and test on MIT-CS2PD.
Fold 2: Train on MIT-CS2PD and test on MIT-CS1PD.

A random split may place similar study-specific patterns in both training and testing. Cross-cohort evaluation asks whether the signal transfers to a different study cohort.

Safeguards: subject-disjoint splits, no session leakage, held-out cohort used only for testing, and subject-level bootstrap confidence intervals.

SLIDE 12 — BASELINE RESULTS
---------------------------
Show results/figures/roc_macd.png.

AUC: 0.8505.
Bootstrap 95% CI: 0.7577–0.9290.

The bootstrap interval was calculated by repeatedly resampling subjects with replacement and recomputing AUC. It describes uncertainty in the estimated AUC, not the range of performance for individual subjects.

Because the sample is relatively small, the interval is wide. The result is not proof of clinical readiness.

SLIDE 13 — MODEL AND FEATURE BENCHMARK
-------------------------------------
Feature/model and mean held-out fold AUC:

MACD + random forest: 0.8650.
MACD + logistic regression: 0.8513.
MACD + standard deviation + logistic regression: 0.8451.
MACD + standard deviation + mean hold + logistic regression: 0.8382.
Standard deviation + logistic regression: 0.8321.
Mean hold time + logistic regression: 0.7481.

The small random forest using MACD alone was the exploratory leader. Only two cross-cohort folds are available, several models were compared, the random forest was selected after the benchmark, and no independent external test set has confirmed it.

Use the phrase promising exploratory candidate, not proven improvement.

SLIDE 14 — EXPLAINABILITY AND MODEL CHOICE
------------------------------------------
Logistic regression is easy to explain, has one coefficient, has a direct relationship between MACD and score, is easy to export to JSON, and is easy to reproduce in JavaScript.

Random forest can capture nonlinear relationships and had higher exploratory AUC, but it is more complex, can overfit a small dataset, is harder to explain in a browser, and needs stronger validation.

Retain logistic regression as the paper replication and transparent baseline. Treat random forest as exploratory until independently validated.

SLIDE 15 — FUTURE WEBAPP PLAN
-----------------------------
Browser keydown/keyup events -> hold-time extraction -> JavaScript MACD -> exported model JSON -> research screening score.

The webapp should capture timing only, not typed text; ignore repeated keydown events; require enough observations; warn when values are outside the training range; show a research-only disclaimer; and never provide a diagnosis.

The Python and JavaScript MACD implementations should remain equivalent through parity tests.

SLIDE 16 — LIMITATIONS
----------------------
Dataset limitations: small sample, only two cohorts, clinic-collected data, limited device and typing-context representation, and possible study-specific effects.

Modeling limitations: only two cross-cohort folds, exploratory benchmark, no independent external test set, wide confidence intervals, and need for stronger validation.

Deployment limitations: browser timing differs from the PhysioNet logger, device differences may affect hold times, medication status may affect timing, and the project must not guide clinical decisions.

SLIDE 17 — CONCLUSION
---------------------
1. EDA supports the feature motivation: PD subjects showed higher average hold times, larger consecutive timing changes, and higher MACD in both cohorts.
2. The MACD baseline is interpretable and reproducible, with AUC approximately 0.8505 under cross-cohort evaluation.
3. The random forest is a promising exploratory candidate, with mean held-out fold AUC 0.8650, but needs stronger validation before being considered a confirmed improvement.

Final statement: The contribution is not only a model score. It is a reproducible, explainable, leakage-aware pipeline connecting EDA motivation to feature engineering, evaluation, benchmarking, and future web deployment.
